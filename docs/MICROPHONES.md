# Microphone evidence and validation boundaries

Checked against manufacturer material and original dataset releases on 2026-09-29. Published specifications are not measurements made by this project. A physically motivated simulation can pass conservation and numerical checks while sounding unlike the hardware. Device equivalence requires independent recordings with measured excitation, geometry and acquisition gain.

## Which hardware is being modeled?

| Device | Acoustic structure and capsule | Published response / sensitivity | Published noise and level limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Neumann KU100](https://www.neumann.com/en-us/products/microphones/ku-100) | Two omni condenser systems in artificial ears on a head-shaped enclosure; diffuse-field equalized | 20 Hz–20 kHz; 20 mV/Pa at 1 kHz | 16 dBA self-noise; 78 dBA SNR; 135 dB SPL at 0.5% THD, 145 dB with pad |
| [3Dio Free Space](https://3diosound.com/products/free-space-binaural-microphone) | Silicone ears on two discs joined by a support; matched 10 mm electret capsules; white ears/discs in current photo | 60 Hz–20 kHz, with upper-band rolloff; −28 ±3 dBV/Pa, approximately 39.8 mV/Pa | SNR 80 dB at 1 kHz; 122 dB SPL typical at 3% distortion |
| [3Dio Free Space XLR](https://3diosound.com/collections/fs-series-microphones/products/free-space-xlr-binaural-microphone) | Same published capsule dimensions and acoustic specifications as Free Space; adds balanced XLR outputs | 60 Hz–20 kHz; −28 ±3 dBV/Pa | SNR 80 dB; 122 dB SPL at 3% distortion |
| [3Dio Free Space Pro II](https://3diosound.com/products/free-space-pro-binaural-microphone) | Black ears/discs in current official photo, badge “FS Pro II”; current introduction names matched DPA 4060 CORE+ capsules | 20 Hz–20 kHz, stated soft +3 dB boost at 8–20 kHz; 20 mV/Pa ±3 dB | Published table: 71 dBA SNR, corresponding to 23 dBA noise for a 94 dB reference; 134 dB peak before clipping |

“Black 3Dio” is insufficient identification: the main support is black on the standard Free Space too. The all-black ear/disc model pictured by the manufacturer is Pro II. Record the badge, capsule generation, serial number, output and filter setting before choosing a profile. The Pro II page's CORE+ introduction and an older CORE frequency plot in support material should not be combined into a supposedly measured current-device transfer curve. The listed limits also use different distortion criteria, so the numbers do not form a like-for-like maximum-SPL ranking.

KU100 provides linear/40/150 Hz low-cut choices and a −10 dB pad. Free Space models offer a 160 Hz rolloff; on XLR and Pro II the manufacturer says that switch affects the 3.5 mm path only. Capture settings therefore matter. Overall manufacturer dimensions (KU100 180 ×280 ×220 mm; 3Dio 190 ×110 ×110 mm) are enclosure dimensions, not verified ear spacing, canal dimensions or contact geometry.

[3Dio's technical support](https://3diosound.com/community/forum/topic/165873/capsule-noise-specs-application) explains that the apparent 14-versus-23 dB self-noise advantage of the standard capsules is affected by their narrower high-frequency response. Equal bandwidth is required for a meaningful comparison. The same reply identifies the illustrated standard capsule as Primo EM272J and the illustrated Pro capsule as DPA 4060 CORE; these bare-capsule curves exclude pinna/canal acoustics. Do not add synthetic device noise merely to satisfy a single scalar specification without a measured spectrum and gain reference.

## Geometry and EQ

[Neumann's interview with its former development director](https://newsroom.neumann.com/the-man-behind-the-neumann-ku-100-dufytw) describes hand-matching the KU100 ears toward mirror symmetry and changing their pitch angle to improve perceived elevation. It also explains the move to a shortened canal and diffuse-field equalization in its predecessor. Thus an arbitrary human ear scan plus a generic canal is not KU100 geometry. No dimensional CAD, mechanical modulus or contact-transfer calibration was established by this research.

The 3Dio disc-and-ear assembly does not reproduce the KU100 head enclosure. [The manufacturer says it offers no calibration files](https://3diosound.com/community/forum/topic/88614/does-3dio-offer-a-calibration-file), and [its pinna-swap discussion](https://3diosound.com/community/forum/topic/202577/changing-the-pinnae) confirms that adapting KU100 ears would require a custom mechanical design. Its [silicone answer](https://3diosound.com/community/forum/topic/218063/silicone-typegrade) supplies no stiffness, damping, thickness or frequency-dependent mechanical data. A color, pinna outline, capsule spec or sensitivity offset cannot convert a KU100 HRTF into a measured 3Dio profile.

Measured HRTFs already contain the measured receiver's geometry and transfer characteristics. Applying another generic ear-canal/head-shadow response would count those effects again. A diffuse-field or headphone compensation stage must have an explicit measurement/reference convention; do not assume the separately offered compensation filters should automatically be appended.

The [near-field measurement paper](https://zenodo.org/api/records/4297951/files/Arend_TMT2016.pdf/content) is particularly relevant: the authors replace the unreliable low-frequency portion with adaptive extension at a 200 Hz crossover, compensate source response, then retain 128 taps. They caution about head–speaker reflections at 0.25 m and robot-arm shadowing in spherical measurements; the circular measurements avoid the robot arm. Consequently, a low-frequency-dominated contact output is not experimentally validated by convolving with this bank. Nor does a match to coefficients used by the renderer independently establish source realism.

## What today's checks establish

The native bank preparer establishes exact provenance, verified channel orientation, once-only distance scaling, retention of all published samples, and reproducible held-out interpolation error. The five circles provide real distance-dependent airborne cues. Log-radius coefficient interpolation is visibly less accurate when a circle is withheld; reported errors must remain available. Use measured radii for strict regression references.

An independent SADIE comparison should preserve each dataset's channel order and metadata, compare common directions, report ILD/ITD and spectral differences, and fit at most an explicitly reported common recording gain/time offset. The different source distances, compensation procedures and hardware sessions must be reported. A single fitted spectral equalizer that absorbs the discrepancy cannot then count as independent validation.

That comparison is now reproducible with `scripts/benchmark_sadie.py --sadie /path/to/D1_48K_24bit_256tap_FIR_SOFA.sofa`; results are in [sadie-benchmark.json](../data/sadie-benchmark.json). At 360 common horizontal directions and a 1.2 m source radius, a single common +16.33 dB offset leaves median spectral RMS disagreement of 2.72 dB (95th percentile 6.16 dB), and frequency-dependent ILD RMS disagreement of 4.17 dB. Median correlation-ITD disagreement is 7.81 µs (95th percentile 55.08 µs). The two sessions agree on lateral orientation; they do not produce identical transfer functions. SADIE's actual SOFA metadata explicitly records low-frequency extension and diffuse-field equalization. TH Köln's 1.2 m comparison response is interpolated between measured circles. These differences remain visible in the report and are not folded into runtime calibration.

For contact equivalence, acquire new measurements: identify the exact microphone unit and filters; record both ears with calibrated ADC gain; measure force, contact location, velocity, indentation and material; repeat dry taps, presses and strokes before introducing controlled film conditions; retain quiet/no-contact recordings and an independent airborne source measurement. Split repetitions/locations into fit and held-out conditions. Compare force-to-voltage transfer, bilateral coupling, spectra, temporal envelopes and repeatability. This protocol is a proposed experiment, not data already obtained.

FAIR-Play can legally serve as attributed reference audio under its declared license, but source performance, room, distance and gain confound a device transfer comparison. Public manufacturer demos do not automatically grant redistribution rights. No reference performance recording is needed or decoded by the native runtime, and no measured contact transfer was found in the examined primary releases.
