NEW SOUND FAMILIES / MEASURED OBJECT RESPONSES

Wood. Ceramic. Glass.

Three real objects, three newly generated tapping sequences. Start with Shorter contact pulse. Switch to Longer contact pulse for the same sequence with a softer excitation. These are different fitted object models—not the ear texture pitched up or down.

Data-assisted synthesis, not recording playback. RealImpact recordings were used to identify resonances, decay and, for wood/glass, a generated transient-noise layer. No recorded waveform is loaded to render these sounds. All outputs now use the shared measured KU100 binaural receiver at 25 cm from the head center. The longer tapping sequences move right to left; the short diagnostic prior stays to the right. This is an approximate transplantation of a recording-colored source—not a fully identified object-to-microphone field or an ASMR acceptance claim.

What the real-world test found

The initial unmeasured oscillator presets did not reproduce these objects. Fitting 24 damped modes improved the response. Wood and glass needed an additional statistical transient component; it generates new noise, not a saved residual recording.

Each model was fitted at one microphone position, then compared at three other distances. Seven of nine development cases met our declared spectral/decay diagnostic limits. The ceramic mug and glass goblet still failed at the farthest position. This is not a 7/9 realism score or a blind listener test.

“Shorter” and “longer” refer to two prescribed pulse widths, not identified hammer materials. Both use the same impulse strengths and gain for each object; the longer pulse can be quieter. The prior is a separate illustrative unmeasured model, not a full geometry simulation.

Exactly what is and is not simulated

The damped oscillator part is a reduced-order dynamical model. Its coefficients are identified from the authors’ force-deconvolved microphone response, so they also contain unseparated radiation, mounting and recording effects. The transient-noise layer is an empirical approximation. We have not recovered absolute newtons-to-pascals calibration, scanned geometry, a full sound field, or wet-ear mechanics.

Raw source recordings and the original ear recordings are not included on this page. All nine downloads are freshly computed PCM16, 48 kHz, two distinct measured-ear channels, including the complete receiver tail. They are pre-rendered; selecting a variant is not an interactive physics calculation.
