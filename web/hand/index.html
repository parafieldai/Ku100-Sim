What is different in this comparison?

Pinch + rub: gripping and motion change the contact forces. Light pinch: the same lateral gesture with less indentation—not a quieter playback setting. Rate-independent friction: the same motion without the assumed velocity-weakening term. Hold: no lateral rubbing; a quiet control, not a failed audio player.

All four use the same native geometric solver and one shared listening gain. No periodic texture ridges, noise track or force-to-volume knob is added. Existing binaural receiver measurements do not calibrate direct ear contact.

Sound and simulation limits

Each mesh face contributes calculated normal volume motion. Six grouped source signals approximate the radiated field and pass through the distinct KU100 ear responses at 25 cm from the head center. This is not a boundary-element acoustic solution, a 25 cm ear gap or a measured hand-on-ear recording.

Mesh refinement and held-out recording tests remain separate. The interface does not turn a force or energy test into an ASMR rating. Controls choose precomputed physical runs; they do not secretly edit the audio or claim an interactive rerender.
